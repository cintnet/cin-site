How distant seismographs verified rivals before inspectors shook hands

Metering giant frontier training clusters like Cold War nuclear seismographs works without placing whitelisted chip locks inside household devices. Separating heavy cluster verification from everyday local AI tools slows risky runs without building global censorship.

Long before American and Soviet weapons inspectors walked side by side beside missile silos in 1987, continental seismographs verified nuclear limits from afar whenever underground rock shook with megaton force. Nobody placed a government padlock on every village printing press or hardware store to detect when a rival tested an H-bomb underground.

Today's debate over slowing frontier AI training often forgets that distinction. In a panel hosted by the Existential Risk Observatory, David Krueger defended The Feasibility of a Hardwired Pause of Frontier AI Training. Twenty-six researchers including Stuart Russell and Barry Eichengreen co-authored that October 2026 working paper.1,2

Where the hardwired pause helps, and where it overreaches

Krueger's team proposes halting risky new AI training by targeting tight bottlenecks in advanced semiconductor manufacturing.1,2 Their blueprint retires general training chips under an international hardware census. Production shifts instead to specialized "inference-only" chips that run existing models without training larger successors.

That supply-chain lens captures an obvious physical truth. Running an already-built model takes a fraction of the power and chip-to-chip bandwidth required to train a brand-new frontier system across massive clusters.

Recent November 2026 training disclosures sharpen both sides of that divide. In a WorldofAI dispatch on frontier pre-training progress, reporting on OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki's update showed how steep 2026 pre-training loss curves still depend on massive compute concentration.8 At the same time, a bycloud briefing on dynamic byte chunking showed compact local models reaching three-billion-parameter benchmark parity with ten times less training data, well below industrial cluster thresholds.8

Wait, though, before shrinking whitelisted inference-only licenses down into everyday laptops and local clinics. Family 5 warned earlier against entrenched totalitarianism. Hardware needing central approval before running local code hands rulers a kill-switch over science, education, and dissent.1

Three missing pieces flagged by fellow panelists

Fellow panelists raised three practical constraints that matter just as much as foundry choke points:

  • Verification works even without friendship. Buck Shlegeris noted that existential stakes between 1945 and 1987 never forced automatic trust. Non-cooperative sensors still detected giant builds decades before on-site inspectors arrived.1
  • Don't throw away useful headroom out of panic. Malo Bourgon noted engineers have five to ten years of useful work deploying today's proven AI systems cleanly. That gives builders room without chasing blind self-improving runs.1
  • Watch asymmetric slowdowns. Shlegeris warned that unilateral pauses fail if rivals catch up unseen. Bourgon noted Chinese labs encounter similar model quirks and already share narrow technical findings informally.1

Seismic limits for factories, open keys for homes

The clean engineering fix splits computing infrastructure in two by physical scale and keeps global firmware blacklists off personal gear.

That division echoes lessons from environmental treaties and avionics alike. Hardware checks belong at transformer yards, optical switches, and flagship chip foundries, metering capacity without opening private text.3,4

At the same time, keep proven local weights legal on off-the-shelf citizen chips. Clinics, co-ops, and public schools can then apply Bourgon's five-to-ten-year wave of useful tools without creating a central censor.1

Steps that work before any global treaty is signed

  1. Deploy external safety seismographs today. Independent evaluators and international safety institutes can publish standardized hazard receipts and anomaly feeds before binding pauses exist.
  2. Keep public compute commons open at human scale. Fund regional shared clusters below treaty scale so rural hospitals and local co-ops avoid mega-vendor lock-in, as detailed in Public computing, explained.
  3. Write hard threshold lines into hardware rules. Compute-governance statutes should separate industrial training fabrics clearly from ordinary consumer accelerators.
