In forty-eight hours before Thanksgiving, the AI timeline stopped feeling theoretical. Frontier labs pushed Lean-verified proofs straight to GitHub with no press releases, and Google DeepMind co-founder Shane Legg moved his 17-year forecast for Minimal AGI to 65 percent before 2028.1,2

If minimal AGI is roughly fourteen months away, cryptographic agility, scoped agent permissions, and crash-proof economic plumbing are no longer ten-year side projects. They are urgent engineering work.

Last night, theoretical computer scientist Scott Aaronson published a post titled, verbatim, HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT.1 Readers who follow Aaronson know he is not a hype salesman. He spent twenty years pouring cold water on exaggerated quantum and AI claims.

He and his colleagues were sitting around a dinner table trying to process OpenAI and Anthropic's Lean-verified proofs of VP ≠ VNP. That drop already landed right behind the Alpöge and Anthropic proof of the Riemann Hypothesis. Before dessert arrived, OpenAI uploaded a proof claiming the exact matrix multiplication exponent is 9/4, including a matching lower bound above 2. During dessert, another drop pinned down the exact linear-gate complexity of the Fourier Transform with a matching lower bound too.

Notice the detail that really gets under your skin. The labs did not hold keynotes or hire PR firms. They just pushed papers and Lean certificates straight to GitHub while researchers sat over plates of food refreshing laptops to see which sixty-year-old wall fell next.

Then this morning, Shane Legg logged onto X.2 For seventeen straight years since 2009, Legg held one calm anchor: a 50 percent chance of human-level AGI by 2028. Today he typed two dry replies to a question in his mentions. First: "Actually, yes. I now think it's over 50% likely that we'll have Minimal AGI before 2028. We're getting close now." Minutes later: "ATM, I'd estimate 65%."

Read those two events side by side. One of the field's sharpest complexity skeptics just wrote that this week feels like the start of a singularity in hard math. One of the field's most patient forecasters just moved a 17-year clock to 65 percent before 2028.

Drop the polite denial

For all I care, you can hate the labs for bulldozing the academic garden. Or you can feel genuine awe, like Galileo pointing a lens at Jupiter, as a new continent of mathematical truth comes into focus. Both reactions make total human sense.

What no longer makes any sense at all is pretending this is still autocomplete.

Aaronson quoted a friend who nailed the irony. For decades, crackpots claimed they solved Millennium Problems. Now crackpots hunt for a typo in a Lean file to claim Millennium Problems were not solved. Humans will need years to unpack these proofs, and individual lemmas still get audited. Still, debating whether the tide is real while water fills the room is not rigor. It is coping.

Two bombshells hiding behind the math celebration

Most commentary this week is stuck either cheering the theorems or worrying about math departments. Two sharper shocks are sitting right in plain sight inside the exact same forty-eight hours.

First: every algorithm breakthrough cuts both ways on cryptography. Aaronson dropped a line that deserves ten times more attention than it got. Faster upper bounds do not just beat the 1960s Fast Fourier Transform. He wrote that he is virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources, that frontier labs are sitting on algorithmic breaks affecting deployed cryptosystems.1

Forget speculation about anyone's crypto wallet for a second. Think about what deployed public-key math holds up: bank settlement, software updates, government portals, hospital records, and standing API keys across the web. When AI-accelerated algorithm discovery chews through hardness assumptions faster than standards committees rotate keys, permanent credentials become a ticking liability.

Second: "before 2028" is fourteen months away, not ten years. Policy debates usually assume a leisurely decade of white papers and committee hearings. Shane Legg's 65 percent estimate covers Minimal AGI: an agent handling typical human cognitive tasks across logic, learning, and multi-step work without major gaps.2,3 If he is even roughly right, that ten-year cushion just vanished.

Look at the macroeconomic reality underneath. Modern states fund roughly three quarters of their budgets off wage taxes. Businesses fund daily working capital off commercial credit tied to jumpy equity collateral. Public institutions still use human typing speed as their spam filter. None of that plumbing was built for general cognitive labor costing pennies an hour inside fourteen months.

What you actually do at the top of the rollercoaster

Aaronson ended his post with something disarmingly human. Spend real time with your kids, parents, and friends this week, and work to make sure there is a world we want to live in at the bottom of this rollercoaster.1

Skip the conference-panel jargon and say it straight. Nobody is going to stop automated mathematical discovery with stern op-eds, and curing disease or settling ancient theorems is not the enemy. The danger is letting a 1998 web and a leveraged casino absorb a 2028 cognitive shock with zero circuit breakers.

Four engineering jobs move from future plans to urgent work this month:

  • Kill standing credentials now. Move automated agents off human logins onto scoped, expiring capability leases that drain to zero automatically. If a cryptosystem or prompt breaks tomorrow, the blast radius stays tiny and revocable.
  • Build crypto-agile, locally verifiable roots. Stop betting personal records and civic receipts on one cloud key or one unbroken hardness assumption. Hardware-anchored local vaults, hash-based post-quantum signatures, and independent cross-checking survive algorithmic surprises.
  • Separate safety verification from lab clocks. When labs push world-historic proofs straight to GitHub without waiting for review, internal self-grading is theater. Independent cross-lineage airlocks have to sit outside the vendor's cluster.
  • Decouple daily livelihoods from leveraged tickers. When white-collar disruption and capex debt collide, equity markets will whip violently. Multilateral clearing, bounded mutual credit, and diffused capital ownership keep essential goods moving so states can pause a risky model without starving citizens.
