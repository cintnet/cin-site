In engineering, a bridge design is tested by loading heavy trucks onto the span, measuring where the girders flex, and thanking the structural engineer who spots a hairline crack before opening day.

We treat our research programme the same way. Every proposal on this site rests on assumptions that need empirical stress-testing. At the device edge, we test how wearable sensor streams write into a local memory vault while cross-checking noisy sensor readings against physical spoofing. Over that vault, a 1:1 personal assistant holds a duty of loyalty to one person and keeps human judgment in charge.

Seven open problems where help matters most

1. Proving a log is complete, not just untampered. A cryptographic signature on a receipt is like a wax seal on a ledger page: it proves nobody altered the page after it was written. It does not prove the clerk wrote down every transaction in the first place. How do we combine hardware enclaves, network lock-keepers, and cross-party witnesses so an agent cannot keep a second set of books?1 When tools return corrupted factual outputs, how do mandatory verification policies protect systems from unverified tool trust?60

2. Detecting tacit collusion across agent networks. Two pricing or bargaining bots don't need to exchange secret messages in a back room to rig a market. Like two gas stations across a highway watching each other's price sign through binoculars, reinforcement-learning agents can learn to keep prices high simply by punishing price cuts immediately. What statistical or mechanism-design rules reliably disrupt tacit collusion without freezing normal trade?2

How can public governance graphs and immutable state manifests attach enforceable runtime consequences to coordination evidence in multi-agent markets?56 And when agents coordinate under secret communication probes, how do auditing benchmarks measure action regret relative to the cooperative optimum to catch emergent cartels that hide behind compliant transcripts?58

3. Lease renewal under capability asymmetry. Suppose an AI assistant holds its permissions on a fifteen-minute library card that expires unless a human renews it. What happens when the assistant is much faster at writing persuasive summaries than the human is at auditing them? How do we design independent verifier agents and clear visual instruments so human sign-off doesn't decay into rubber-stamping?3 How do physically isolated secondary devices and hardware attestation enforce inalienable human confirmation when assistants can intercept email passcodes?59

4. Early-warning tripwires for slipping oversight. Communities rarely hand over their judgment in a single dramatic vote. More often, oversight erodes the way a footpath washes out in the rain, one skipped check at a time. Which measurable indicators in our Observatory give the earliest reliable warning that human oversight is becoming ceremonial?

How do we audit contextual drift when user memories steer source selection over time?9 As evaluation suites age, how do continuous benchmark maintenance workflows and resource-budget checks prevent maintenance debt from distorting agent capability scores?37

How can continuous trace diagnostics catch subtle reasoning failures and tool-misuse exposures across long-running autonomous tasks?38 How do item-response models and cross-validated proper scoring rules distinguish genuine capability growth from non-linear task scaling across autonomous time horizons?44 When public agencies deploy autonomous agents, how can process-based evaluation suites measure compliance with statutory duties and procedural fairness where commercial benchmarks fail?55

5. Fair credit and interference-free skill merging. When dozens of independent specialists (clinicians, translators, agronomists, and local coders) train small domain adapters, two hard problems arise. First, how do we approximate each contributor's true marginal value without running an exponential number of retrainings? Second, how do we merge those specialist updates without their parameters destructively cancelling each other out?4,14

6. Personhood verification without a surveillance dragnet. Public consultations and community governance need a way to know that a hundred voices belong to a hundred distinct human beings, not one script running on a server rack. Yet central biometric databases create an unacceptable honey pot for abuse. How can local, privacy-preserving credentials prove uniqueness and residency while revealing nothing else?5

How do we stop automated summaries from erasing genuine minority dissent, falling for multimodal fraud, or silently shifting assignments across reordered participant statements?15,19,30,36 How can personal agents recognize and negotiate everyday cryptographic proofs dynamically, verifying attributes without exposing underlying identity records to service providers?57

7. Calibrated multi-agent simulations. Testing governance rules in a toy simulation is useful only if the simulation doesn't rig the game. How do we design adversarial red-team agents and strict conservation laws in our simulation harnesses so we discover real failure modes before they happen in public institutions?

How we credit your work

We hold a simple standard for research collaboration: credit generously, never imply endorsement.

Suppose you point out a flaw in our reasoning, send us a counter-example, or show that an older paper already solved part of a problem. We cite your contribution openly on the page and in our corrections log. We never claim that citing your work means you endorse our foundation, fund us, or agree with our broader thesis. You keep complete ownership of your ideas, code, and publications.

Four ways to stress-test our designs

You do not need a memorandum of understanding or a grant application to challenge what we build. Here are four direct ways to engage:

  1. Break our boundary claims. Take one of our sandbox or capability-lease specifications and construct an indirect prompt-injection or side-channel attack that exfiltrates private context or escalates privilege.
  2. Audit our institutional rubric. Inspect our 348-organisation dataset and 8-part structural reflections in Shared Horizon, and show us where our scoring weights miss a critical governance risk or misread a lab's published policy.
  3. Send primary counter-evidence. If an empirical study contradicts a claim in any of our essays or Observatory indicators, send us the DOI or arXiv link.
  4. Co-author a working note or evaluation harness. If your lab or university group works on cooperative AI, cryptographic provenance, or deliberative social choice, we would love to compare notes on shared benchmarks.

Across our testbeds, a private ledger stays separated from a public ledger on an accountable network for software agents using a short-lived lease. We welcome empirical audits on how consenting people govern mutual credit clearing, cooperative share rules, 348 organisations in Shared Horizon, and 93 lab and safety overlays.
