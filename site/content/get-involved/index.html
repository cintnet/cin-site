Six practical civic and technical paths test personal agent safeguards, local memory vaults, and collective rules. A town keeps its drinking water clean and its streets safe for children walking to school through shared work. It never rests on one hero in a laboratory. The chemist tests the water sample, the plumber fits the valve, the town council writes the plumbing code, and the parent notices when a hydrant is leaking on the corner.

Keeping AI understandable and accountable works the same way. Each role tests a different piece of the plumbing, from wearable sensors and a local memory vault that checks for spoofed sensor readings to a 1:1 personal assistant on your side.

Policy makers

For officials, staffers and regulators who want practical procurement rules, plain-language briefings and honest evidence.

Researchers

For people working on AI safety, alignment, governance or collective intelligence who can test our assumptions and point out what we've missed.

AI labs

For researchers, engineers, safety teams and policy staff inside labs who want outside scrutiny and shared safety standards.

Builders

For engineers and open-source developers who want to turn ideas like verifiable receipts into working tools people can inspect.

Civil society

For co-operatives, credit unions, worker associations, tenant organisers and community clinics defending collective self-determination.

Everyone

For citizens, parents, workers, co-ops, unions, newsrooms and neighbours who want AI that answers to the people it affects.

How the six roles reinforce each other

Often, people in one role feel stuck because they are waiting on someone else. A policy staffer worries that requiring receipts on AI actions might be technically impossible. An engineer inside a lab knows receipts are easy to build, but worries management won't prioritise them unless customers and procurement officers ask for them.

When each group takes one practical step in the open, the logjam breaks:

  • Builders prove a safeguard works in clean, open-source code so nobody can claim it is impossible.
  • Researchers test that safeguard under adversarial pressure to find where it bends or breaks.
  • Lab teams adopt shared workload identities and receipt formats so safety doesn't punish the first mover.
  • Policy makers write those proven receipts and appeal rights into public procurement contracts.
  • Civil-society groups pool member demand across co-ops, clinics, and unions so nobody bargains alone.
  • Households and citizens ask a simple question before adopting any tool: who does this assistant answer to, and can we see the receipt?

Across our designs, a household's private ledger stays at home so human judgment stays in charge, while outward errands post selective signed proofs to a public ledger on an accountable network for software agents. Short-lived permission leases and physical cutoff relays bound every tool call, while consenting people coordinate mutual credit, cooperative share rules, 348 organisations in Shared Horizon, and 93 lab overlays.

Where things honestly stand

We are at the beginning of this work. We want to be plain with you about what is ready today and what remains ahead.

Open now

  • Hard questions and critique of our work.
  • Collaboration on open research problems.
  • Pointers to prior work or things we've missed.
  • Early review of drafts before we publish them.
  • Interactive exploration in Shared Horizon and the Observatory.

Not yet

  • Closed commercial cloud subscriptions or advertising auctions.
  • Donations, grants or outside investment.
  • Open jobs or paid roles.
  • Anything for sale, including tokens (we will never sell those).

Open code, benchmarks, and public datasets live under github.com/cintnet. For open discussion, reading groups, and project questions, join our Discord community.

If you don't fit neatly into any box above, write to us anyway at contact@collectiveintelligencenetwork.org. A person reads every message, and the best insights often come from people living with these systems firsthand.

For researchers

Across all six civic and technical roles, our open architecture unites five functional primitives. First, across the multimodal egocentric and ambient perceptual sensorium, hardware-timed (Device Time) peripheral silicon attestation with self-assessed observation confidence (PUF, TCG DICE, DMTF SPDM, and IETF RATS under RFC 93341) pairs with an optical data diode (optical data diodes) and physical dead-man safety relays (IEC 61508). Second, a local append-only C2PA and Merkle episodic memory vault (Endel Tulving; Tulving, 1972)1 enforces transduction realism (Signed Is Not True across the analog hole; Thompson, 1984; Kevin Fu and Wenyuan Xu; Fu & Xu, 20181). Third, a monogamous 1:1 personal cognitive model (cybernetic exocortex; Joscha Bach; Bach; Conant & Ashby, 1970, Good Regulator theorem; Andy Clark and David Chalmers; Clark & Chalmers, 1998, Extended Mind) acts as a statutory information fiduciary (Jack Balkin; Balkin, 20162) that preserves causal do(x) unassisted competence (Pearl, 20092). Fourth, bifurcated provenance ledgers split the Private Autobiographical Ledger from the Public Attestation & Escrow Ledger (Public Attestation and Escrow Ledger) across a personal Markov blanket (Karl Friston; Friston, 2010; Friston, 20132) using selective zero-knowledge (ZK) proofs, expiring Object-Capability leases (capability lease; Jack B. Dennis and Earl C. Van Horn; Dennis & Van Horn, 19662), IETF SCITT transparency receipts (RFC 99431), and independent-class k >= 3f + 1 Byzantine corroboration. Fifth, on the polycentric collective coordination substrate, sovereign dyads couple to the Wide Network Ledger for Universal Basic Services, merit-bonded tasks, reciprocal mutual credit (James Stodder; Stodder, 2009), and invisible maintenance recognition (Jacob Meluso; Meluso et al., 2024). It also coordinates frontier-lab overlays and statutory cooperative equity pools (Elinor Ostrom; Ostrom, 19903; Rudolf Meidner; Meidner, 19783) while bounding multi-agent strategic failures (Bracale Syrnikov et al., 20263), verifiable in /labs/protocol-inspector/, /labs/sensor-boundary/, /labs/shared-horizon/, and /labs/sovereign-papers/.
