Think of the electrical code and municipal water charter written before high-voltage lines and mains enter a town. Residents do not wait for a transformer fire to decide where circuit breakers belong, who owns the neighbourhood pipes, or whether a private operator can shut off drinking water during a dispute.

Capable software agents and automated assembly lines need the same technical ground rules in daylight. The Collective Intelligence Network does not pre-legislate how communities live. Instead, this draft charter (v1.0) binds our own single-user agent harness, physical safety airlocks, and shared transparent ledger above nineteen channel-integrity seals.

Preamble: why write a charter before the concrete sets?

Software agents already write code, negotiate purchases, dispatch freight, and steer industrial equipment faster than any human committee can review line by line. Waiting until automated systems lock in concentrated control leaves families and towns renting their daily tools from distant operators.

At the same time, CIN must never dictate societal bylaws from above. Living people and their personal agents must decide how they wish to be governed. Every article below binds our own agent harness, state-of-the-art physical airlocks, and shared transparent ledger so the coordination wire stays honest.

On an unrigged wire, each person's single-user exploratory agent can coordinate high-fidelity, privacy-protected lived signal across daily life. People and their agents then discover common ground and shape their own evolving policies, while we cross-test these safeguards openly with AI labs and safety institutes.

Article I: Non-negotiable human rights and hard red lines

Article I incorporates all nineteen channel-integrity seals as strict first-order restraints on our agent harness and ledger wire. Living communities and their agents build and revise their own substantive policies freely above this calibrated floor, and no shortcut or emergency drops below it.

  • Life, non-force, and bodily autonomy. Software and connected hardware never use, direct, or authorize weapons, physical force, or coercion against human beings.
  • Local data custody and narrow proofs. You can inspect, export, and delete your personal records on your own devices. Verifiers ask only for proof of a specific fact without collecting raw personal histories, and refusing to share data never penalizes you.
  • Fair process and zero human ranking. Any restriction requires clear evidence, advance notice, and a human appeal. Doubt falls on the system rather than the citizen, and no tool computes a score of human worth.
  • Attention, rest, and offline parity. Nothing enters your screen or workspace without your revocable consent. Every person keeps an unconditional right to rest, unhurried play, and full analog or offline access to essential services without penalty.
For researchers and legal drafters (Article I)

Article I enforces constitutional floor rights through strict lexicographic priority over downstream utility optimization, extending Constitutional AI1 and reviewable automated decision-making.2 Selective disclosure relies on interactive and non-interactive zero-knowledge proofs so verifiers check narrow predicates without raw data custody, while social protection baselines follow International Labour Organization Recommendation No. 202.3

Article II: Fiduciary personal assistants and human competence

A personal assistant should work like a licensed doctor or legal counsel bound to your interest, never like a shop clerk paid a hidden commission by manufacturers across the street. At the same time, even a loyal assistant harms you if doing everything for you causes your own judgment to wither.

  • Undivided loyalty to the user. Every personal assistant owes a binding duty of care, confidentiality, and loyalty solely to the person authorizing it, with zero hidden advertising or vendor steering.
  • Barriers against prompt hijacking. Personal tools separate your direct instructions from untrusted emails, web pages, and peer messages so outside files cannot silently trigger your accounts.
  • Forward goals over old click trails. Your assistant learns from the commitments and curiosity you choose for your future rather than trapping you inside past browsing habits.
  • Step-back coaching and solo-flight hours. Tools gradually reduce hints as you master a skill and schedule regular unassisted solo-flight hours so human craft and clinical or civic judgment grow sharper over time.
For researchers and legal drafters (Article II)

Section 2.1 codifies Jack Balkin's information fiduciary duty of care and undivided loyalty at the local runtime layer.4 Section 2.2 enforces instruction-hierarchy isolation and dynamic taint tracking to defeat indirect prompt injection by design.5 Section 2.4 addresses Lisanne Bainbridge's ironies of automation6 through progressive instructional scaffolding decay so longitudinal unassisted human capability is a mandatory audit metric.

Article III: Separate agent networks, open provenance, and expiring leases

When heavy freight trucks share narrow residential lanes without markings, pedestrians get pushed off the road. Automated agent traffic belongs on dedicated, verifiable lanes, and every permission an agent carries should work like a temporary library pass rather than a blank cheque.

  • Separate roads for automated traffic. High-speed machine traffic (MCP and A2A) runs on dedicated, authenticated network channels so human civic spaces stay reserved for real people.
  • Power on loan through expiring leases. Agents hold zero legal personhood. Every permission comes from an identifiable person or cooperative inside a narrow, short-lived capability lease that expires automatically unless renewed.
  • Signed receipts and free exit. Consequential automated actions carry verifiable receipts showing what rule authorized them, and any person or town can move their records to another open provider without penalty.
  • Shared immunity against new exploits. When one local sandbox catches a new scam or prompt attack, it shares a privacy-stripped warning signature so peer sandboxes block the same trick within seconds.
For researchers and legal drafters (Article III)

Agent containment combines Dennis and Van Horn's Object-Capability (OCap) model7 with Gray and Cheriton's fault-tolerant distributed capability leases8 and Necula's Proof-Carrying Code (PCC) certificates.9 Sub-delegation strictly attenuates authority across MCP and A2A sessions over IETF QUIC, while peer sandboxes exchange reproducible zero-knowledge exploit signatures.

Article IV: Pre-deployment safety cases and physical dead-man interlocks

On a railway locomotive or sawmill, safety never rests on software promises alone. A physical dead-man switch cuts power to the wheels the instant the human operator lets go. Machines that move heavy steel, chemicals, or grid switches need the same physical humility.

  • Independent safety cases before release. Any lab or cooperative deploying high-autonomy swarms, industrial control models, or frontier systems approaching general-purpose or human-surpassing capability must pass an independent pre-deployment safety case first.
  • Automatic pauses when oversight lags. Autonomous task duration and tool reach may grow only in step with proven containment. When capability outruns human oversight, scaling pauses automatically.
  • Hardware dead-man relays on physical machines. Robots, autonomous vehicles, and automated factory lines wire independent safety microcontrollers and physical relays directly into motor power lines. Without an active local community heartbeat, hardware cuts power to a safe rest state.
  • Local co-ownership of power and compute. Households and neighbourhoods co-own local solar microgrids, repairable tools, and local compute hardware early so no distant monopoly can pull the plug on local self-rule.
For researchers and legal drafters (Article IV)

Article IV integrates Nancy Leveson's systems-theoretic functional safety engineering10 with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework11 and IEC 61508 hardware safety integrity levels. Physical actuators place isolated safety microcontrollers and analog torque/power relays in series with motor buses, gated by locally attested capability-lease heartbeats.

Article V: Automated production, cooperative ownership, and mutual clearing

If robots and software bake the bread, mill the lumber, and run the clinics while a tiny handful of firms own every machine, wages disappear while bills remain. Peaceful prosperity requires turning automated factories into member-owned cooperatives without violent seizure or central-state bureaucracy.

  • Gradual share conversion into cooperatives. Public automation assessments are paid in newly issued voting shares streamed into producer-consumer cooperatives, uniting builders, maintainers, watershed stewards, and everyday users under shared ownership.
  • Blind task proposals and sunsetting concentrated control. Wealth holders can fund commissioning bonds to propose research or infrastructure projects. Proposals are reviewed with sponsor names hidden, earn non-transferable civic recognition rather than voting power, and steadily convert private equity into cooperative shares.
  • Universal Basic Services plus expiring allocation credits. Every resident receives unconditional baseline food, housing, healthcare, clean energy, education, and local compute without trading personal data, alongside equal, expiring credits for scarce land, minerals, and custom goods.
  • Opt-in durability feedback and interest-free clearing. Members who voluntarily share privacy-protected durability and repairability reports steer next-round manufacturing and earn consumer co-op shares, while cooperatives clear trade on an interest-free, capacity-backed mutual credit ledger.
For researchers and legal drafters (Article V)

Statutory equity conversion adapts Rudolf Meidner's collective share-issuance funds12 into multi-stakeholder producer-consumer cooperatives. Scarce non-baseline capacity clears via periodic allocation credits with predictable demurrage decay, while inter-cooperative trade settles on zero-sum, capacity-backed mutual credit clearinghouses modeled on Switzerland's WIR network13 and Sardinia's Sardex network.14

Article VI: Open equilibrium and containment of secretive shadow ledgers

A fair cooperative network must answer a hard realist question: what happens if outside operators launch secretive agent networks to fix prices, hoard hardware, or dodge safety clutches? The answer combines strong family privacy inside the network with physical meter checks and cartel-breaking incentives at the border.

  • Strong local privacy rings. Families and neighbourhoods prove they follow safety and fairness rules using cryptographic receipts without publishing private conversations, removing any honest reason to flee to a shadow network.
  • Physical energy and material balance checks. Secret agent networks can hide data packets, yet they cannot hide megawatts, silicon chips, cooling heat, or freight tonnage. Participating power grids and fabs publish privacy-preserving input-output meter balances that expose unaccounted dark-compute sinks.
  • Reciprocal club benefits and border margins. Only networks that verify safety and energy balances share zero-fee credit clearing, demand forecasts, and exploit-immunity feeds. Unverified shadow networks pay an automatic quarantine margin at every border trade.
  • Automatic safe harbor for cartel whistleblowers. Secret cartels cannot enforce illegal pacts in public court. The first insider or agent to submit cryptographic proof of covert collusion receives full safe harbor and whistleblower credit, collapsing secret cartels from within.
For researchers and legal drafters (Article VI)

Open-network stability pairs Roger Myerson's cooperative graph value15 with boundary thermodynamic conservation proofs verifying that physical energy inputs match accounted cooperative workloads. Against tacit or covert algorithmic collusion,16 Section 6.4 implements Joseph Harrington's optimal corporate leniency mechanism17 so asymmetric whistleblower safe harbor makes secret shadow-ledger pacts game-theoretically unstable.

Article VII: Polycentric local governance and root-bounded modality standing

Good rules leave everyday decisions closest to the people living with the consequences. A distant global board has no business micromanaging a rural watershed or a neighbourhood tool library, and a million synthetic bots must never outvote a single living citizen.

  • Local self-rule first. Families, housing co-ops, craft guilds, and regional commons set their own local bylaws and budgets above the Article I floor, and any community can fork the open code and leave cleanly.
  • One living human, one equal voice. On constitutional rules and fundamental rights, every verified living person holds one equal vote. Wealth, compute hardware, and past patronage buy zero extra voting weight, and simulated bots never vote.
  • Protected voice for rare lived experience. In safety and needs assessments, ten thousand copycat messages carry diminishing weight. Verified rare human experiences (such as sensory disabilities, rare conditions, or frontline repair trades) keep protected visibility, capped at one real human root per person.
  • Open amendments and public corrections. Amendments to Articles II through VII require ninety days of public review, independent safety checks, and broad democratic ratification, while Article I can never be weakened. See Governance and funding for how our founding scaffolding transitions to public standing.
For researchers and legal drafters (Article VII)

Article VII builds on Elinor Ostrom's polycentric commons governance18 and Gilad Abiri's public constitutional AI framework.19 Needs-discovery and hazard aggregation apply Lu Hong and Scott Page's functional diversity results20 via submodular bridging weights anchored to privacy-preserving personhood credentials,21 preventing synthetic Sybil spoofing while preserving equal per-capita constitutional voting.
