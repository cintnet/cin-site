We started from a simple worry and an equally simple hope. The worry is that AI is growing capable much faster than our public institutions are adapting. Without clear ground rules, ordinary people could lose both the ability to see what software is doing in their name and any real say in how it works. The hope is that, with the right guardrails, the same tools can help communities think better together than any of us could alone.

When cars first shared town streets with people on foot, towns responded with stop signs, licence plates and driving tests. Software that acts for people needs the equivalent rules, and few of them exist yet.

Our work is research and civic engineering for the next set of roads. We ask how a world of capable software agents stays understandable and accountable to the people living in it. We write our proposals in plain language, test them in simulation code, and publish everything openly so you can inspect our reasoning, improve what works, or prove us wrong.

Why an independent foundation?

Most writing about AI today comes from two places. Either it comes from companies selling a product, who naturally want you to believe their software is inevitable and harmless. Or it comes from distant commentators who point out what is broken without building a practical alternative you can actually test.

We wanted a third kind of workshop: more like a municipal water laboratory or a consumer testing kitchen. In a testing kitchen, nobody is paid a commission to praise a toaster. The engineers plug the appliance in, measure whether the cord overheats, write down the results in plain words, and publish the blueprint for a safer plug so any manufacturer can use it.

What we are, and aren't

We are

  • Incorporated in Ottawa, Canada as an independent research foundation.
  • Publishing open specifications, runnable simulations, and reference harnesses for sealed local memory vaults (including noisy or spoofed sensor checks) and 1:1 personal assistants bound to your wishes and wellbeing. Each assistant states honest uncertainty so human judgment stays in charge.
  • Working in the open, with our reasoning, sources, and unsolved problems published.
  • Built with the help of AI agents, with a human steward reviewing and standing behind everything published.
  • Engaging around 40 AI labs and 50 safety groups to map where architectures converge or diverge and test shared safeguards.

We are not

  • Selling closed cloud subscriptions, advertising auctions, or proprietary lock-in.
  • Asking you to stream raw wearable or ambient sensor data to a remote vendor server.
  • Selling tokens, coins, or promising anyone financial returns.
  • Writing top-down rules for how consenting people or local communities must govern themselves.
  • Financially controlled by the labs, agencies, or vendors we evaluate, and citing someone's research never implies endorsement.

How the pieces of our site fit together

If you are visiting for the first time, here is the shortest map of what is on our workbench:

  • Vision explains our guardrails: a local memory vault, a 1:1 assistant loyal to you, and a private ledger kept separate from the public ledger. Outside work runs on a second road for agents using an expiring capability lease, a zero-tool airlock, and public computing.
  • Research separates what is already established in peer-reviewed science from what we propose and what remains unsolved across 69 working papers.
  • Labs houses six live workbenches: Shared Horizon for role moves, the Observatory for scored signposts, AI Lab Overlays, and the Institutional Atlas. You can also test mutual credit and cooperative shares in Simulations or fault-test leases in the Protocol Inspector.
  • Blog and common questions translate technical designs, from medical bill checks and pooled purchasing to expiring permissions, into everyday kitchen-table answers.

Much of this is unsolved, and we say where. The open problems page lists what we haven't worked out and what would help.
