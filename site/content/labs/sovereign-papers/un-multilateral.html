Section 01 · Current Regulatory Architecture & Capture Risks

Unverified communiques, superpower inspection deadlocks, and compute exclusion

Global summits produced the UN Global Digital Compact, the Independent International Scientific Panel on AI, IN-AISI, the G7 Hiroshima Process, and Council of Europe Convention CETS 225. Despite energetic diplomacy, high-level ethical communiques lack technical verification tools1, leaving states reliant on redacted corporate blog posts.

Meanwhile, proposals for intrusive physical inspections hit a geopolitical wall. Rival superpowers refuse to let foreign inspectors copy sovereign frontier model weights or trust firmware keys controlled by a single foreign chip vendor, stalling containment treaties.

Exclusion deepens the fracture. Over 140 Global South countries sit locked out of frontier compute governance2 while importing closed SaaS tools. Meanwhile, no international body governs cross-border agent floods or post-labor wage erosion.

Summit pledges without compute proofs

Non-binding declarations lack megawatt or cryptographic verification, masking sudden jumps in autonomous cyber or biological capabilities1.

Superpower weight-inspection impasse

Major powers reject physical weight handovers and single-vendor hardware monitors on national-security grounds, blocking traditional arms-control inspections.

Global Majority compute lockout

Developing nations face foreign-exchange SaaS drain and brain drain while closed club summits write global frontier rules without them2.

Unsigned transboundary agent floods

Cross-border autonomous swarms share unsigned web channels with human speech while ILO labor conventions lack automation-equity safeguards.

The IETF and ITU signed agent channel and anti-shutdown accord

When a cross-border cyber-agent swarm or billion-bot disinformation wave crosses subsea cables during a crisis, states lacking protocol separation face panic choices. Severing international links or imposing civilian internet blackouts devastates hospitals, trade, and human rights.

Pillar II of CIN's multilateral protocol establishes an IETF and ITU Second Road Accord8. Cross-border autonomous machine swarms must travel over dedicated encrypted channels carrying originating AISI clearances, accountable legal principals, and expiring capability leases6,8.

Internet exchange points and subsea cable operators can then quarantine unattested machine floods at line speed. In exchange, signatory states bind themselves under international human-rights law never to shut down the human internet.
