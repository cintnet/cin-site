Section 01 · Current Regulatory Architecture & Capture Risks

Voluntary federal testing, defense secrecy, and six-month algorithmic catch-up

The United States concentrates the world's largest private frontier clusters across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and xAI. Federal oversight rests on NIST CAISI, OMB M-24-10, and the NSF NAIRR pilot. Yet pre-deployment safety testing at NIST CAISI still depends on voluntary corporate consent while trade groups lobby to block state safety laws.

National-security procurement creates a second capture risk. Classified single-vendor defense contracts can lock frontier superintelligence inside private contractor clearances beyond democratic oversight. Meanwhile, city and state agencies buy opaque commercial tools through legacy procurement pipelines6.

Commerce BIS chip export controls also cannot secure safety alone. Algorithmic progress halves compute needs roughly every eight months, allowing six-to-twelve-month distillation catch-up1. Running unauthenticated bots over the human internet leaves Washington vulnerable to a cyber emergency that triggers panic executive internet lockdowns.

Voluntary NIST testing and preemption

Commercial equity pressure compresses voluntary pre-release testing windows. Corporate lobbying resists binding state safety and liability statutes.

Classified contractor lock-in

Opaque defense cloud contracts risk enclosing frontier capabilities behind private clearances. Local agencies struggle with legacy vendor lock-in6.

Export controls vs. fast distillation

Hardware embargoes buy limited time against algorithmic efficiency gains1. Lab workstation breaches shrink rival catch-up windows to months or hours.

The emergency lockdown crisis trap

Doing nothing until autonomous cyber worms strike invites panic. Presidents then face pressure for emergency cluster seizures and internet identity gates.
