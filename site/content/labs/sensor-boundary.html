A valid hardware signature never guarantees an honest physical reading; cross-checking separate sensors drops failure rates from 81.9 percent to 0.05 percent with zero private diary bytes exposed. Imagine a cold-storage delivery van carrying vaccines across town. A tamper-proof chip on the thermometer signs every reading, yet someone taped a warm coin beside a cool pack right over the probe.

The digital signature is genuine, and the vaccines still spoil. Across physical rooms, roads, and clinics, trusting a single hardware signature fails whenever a signal is faked before digitization, whenever software spots a scheduled inspection, or whenever four cameras share one buggy vendor update.

We ran 2,000 simulated fault trials across eight attack and bias scenarios to test how independent sensor channels, random spot checks, and local diary boundaries hold up together.

What this wind tunnel tests

1. Why a valid chip signature does not prove a physical reading

A cryptographic chip can prove that a message came from a specific camera or thermometer after digitization. When an attacker shines a laser or ultrasonic tone into the analog probe beforehand, the chip signs the distorted measurement anyway.1

Combining separate physical principles catches those upstream tricks. If an optical camera frame claims a delivery pallet moved, local inertial motion and barometric pressure sensors on independent clocks check whether physical acceleration matched.

2. Stopping inspection gaming, vendor monocultures, and unfair bias

Fixed inspection calendars fail whenever software spots test conditions and behaves cleanly only during scheduled audits, a pattern documented in automotive emissions defeat devices.2 Unscheduled spot checks paired with short transfer holds remove the predictable calendar.

Counting four witnesses from the same manufacturer creates a false sense of safety because shared firmware bugs fail together.3 Requiring separate chip, software, and owner lineages stops one broken update from rubber-stamping a false claim. Whenever an honest sensor reports wide uncertainty due to lighting or skin-tone calibration limits,4 the rule withholds automatic penalties and routes the check to contestable human review.

3. Keeping your personal diary sealed on your own device

Verifying public work must never turn everyday life into a fishbowl. Raw audio, video, and health notes stay encrypted inside an append-only local diary readable only by you and your dedicated personal assistant.

When an outside cooperative or buyer needs proof that a delivery arrived safely, your personal assistant shares only a short session-fresh receipt5 and a continuous sequence check. That boundary blocks cherry-picked omissions6 while keeping raw personal diary bytes exposed at exact zero.

Honest limits

Cross-checking separate sensors and hardware lineages raises the cost of physical spoofing sharply, yet it cannot make hardware infallible in an untrusted room. High-stakes medical, safety, and civic decisions still require physical dead-man switches, human appeal paths, and democratic oversight.
