Four checkable safeguards limit tool authority, seal append-only audit trails, cut physical power on stall, and clear mutual credit without exposing household identities. When you check into a hotel, the desk clerk hands you a plastic keycard stamped only for Room 304 until checkout at noon. It will not open Room 305, the kitchen pantry, or the front-desk safe, and after noon the lock rejects it automatically.

You also get two extra protections that do not depend on trusting anyone's memory. At checkout, an itemised folio lists every charge so an unexpected fee stands out right away. Inside the room, a brass deadbolt chain on the doorframe stops anyone outside from pushing the door open, even with a master card.

Software assistants, warehouse arms, and cooperative ledgers need those same checks, plus a way to settle shared work without exposing family names. Pick any of the four protocol envelopes below and flip the fault switches to see which invariant stops each failure.

How the four safeguards fit together

1. Scoped passes replace standing master keys

Permanent login credentials act like a master ring to every room in town. Under capability-based security, authority travels in short-lived passes bound to a named owner, an allow-list of tools, a unit cap, and a cutoff clock.1

Even inside an active pass, untrusted text from an external vendor invoice or email must never steer which tool runs next. Isolating control flow from external documents stops hidden prompt instructions before they reach sensitive tools.2

2. Chained receipts expose silent edits

Self-written summaries can sound reassuring even when an automated tool skipped a check or misread a price list. Each outward step seals SHA-256 digests of its inputs, outputs, and parent receipt.3 Seeded spot-check replays then catch altered or cherry-picked logs right away.3

3. Hardware relays keep final say over physical force

Software checks alone are never enough around heavy machinery, clinic equipment, or warehouse lifters. Multi-version software components still share correlated blind spots under unusual conditions,4 and human operators need direct physical override when automation stalls.5

Layer 3 places an optically isolated hardware relay between compute boards and motor power. If pulses lag past 50 milliseconds, sensors diverge, or a worker hits the stop button, copper contacts cut power locally.

4. Blinded clearing settles shared work without surveillance

When local clinics, couriers, and supply cooperatives clear mutual credit, open auditability must never expose household records.6,7 Layer 4 checks that credits sum to exact zero and blocks duplicate bot nullifiers.6 It also rejects any batch carrying raw names, street addresses, or emails.7

Honest limits

Cryptographic envelopes and hardware relays can prove whether an action stayed inside its signed bounds, left an intact receipt, and respected physical stop switches. They cannot decide whether a task lease was wise or fair to issue in the first place. Democratic governance and human review still set those boundaries.

  • Layer 1 object-capability lease and taint gate: Enforces constant-time HMAC delegation binding over (lease_id, principal_id, delegate_agent_id, allowed_tools, ceiling_units, issued_at_epoch, expires_at_epoch), caps lease lifetime at 86,400 seconds, and rejects invocations where control_origin != "trusted_principal_intent" or tainted_control_tokens is non-empty (Dennis & Van Horn, 1966; Debenedetti et al., 2025).1,2
  • Layer 2 proof-carrying provenance chain: Verifies W3C PROV-style input and output digests alongside parent hash continuity and seeded replay commitments (SHA-256(seed, input_sha256, output_payload)) to catch omitted steps or unfaithful post-hoc rationalisations (Necula, 1997).3
  • Layer 3 optically isolated dead-man interlock: Enforces heartbeat_gap_ms <= 50, independent dual-channel load agreement within 1.5 kg, hardwired E-stop circuit continuity, and hardware latch lockout against network re-energize packets (Knight & Leveson, 1986; Bainbridge, 1983).4,5
  • Layer 4 selective-disclosure mutual-credit clearing: Verifies epoch nullifier uniqueness (SHA-256("nullifier|" || epoch_id || "|" || blinded_root)), exact integer zero-sum conservation (sum(deltas) == 0) compatible with periodic demurrage caps, and recursive key/value PII exclusion under least-disclosure principles (Stodder, 2009; Saltzer & Schroeder, 1975).6,7
