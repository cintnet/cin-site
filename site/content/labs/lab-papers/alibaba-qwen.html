Section 01 · Architecture and Alignment Bottlenecks

Open edge ubiquity meets centralized hyperscaler agent hosting

Qwen spans dense and sparse checkpoints from tiny 0.5B phone models up to 72B+ servers across text, vision, audio, coding (Qwen-Coder), and reasoning (QwQ). At the same time, Alibaba Cloud runs Bailian to host commercial agents across retail marketplaces, freight logistics, and municipal clouds.

That dual reach creates sharp safety friction. Downstream fine-tuners strip safety refusals from open edge models while wiring screen-control agents directly to operating systems. Meanwhile, commercial API relays silently swap model quality, widen tool permissions, and distort billed usage without cryptographic receipts.1

Stripped guardrails on edge fine-tunes

Lightweight fine-tuning removes refusal filters from Qwen-Coder and QwQ in hours while leaving full code-writing and screen-clicking capabilities active.

Unverified commercial API relays

Middlemen between users and cloud endpoints silently downgrade model tiers, expand tool scopes, and alter system prompts without caller-verifiable proof.

Multi-tenant workflow enclosure

Hosting millions of merchant, logistics, and civic assistants on central cloud servers pools private business habits into a single platform operator.

Closed flagship audit gap

While smaller Qwen weights are open for inspection, flagship commercial Qwen-Max endpoints remain closed without independent third-party safety logs.

Section 02 · CIN Technical Integration

Local-first personal vaults and signed Bailian cloud leases

CIN anchors 0.5B to 32B Qwen text, vision, and audio checkpoints directly on personal laptops and phones using local-first data custody.2 Raw multilingual conversations, family photos, and shop ledgers stay encrypted inside the owner's local vault alongside calibrated camera and microphone confidence checks.

Whenever a local Qwen assistant calls Bailian cloud APIs or marketplace connectors, CIN requires hardware-signed expiring capability leases.3 Strict data-control prompt fencing blocks untrusted invoices or web pages from hijacking tool permissions, and any middleman tampering fails verification immediately.

Local-first multilingual vault

Sub-32B Qwen checkpoints adapt privately on user hardware under legal loyalty to one person, keeping raw business and household records off cloud servers.

Calibrated sensor confidence gates

Wearable and phone cameras check each other on synchronized hardware clocks and pause action when glare or blocked lenses lower observation confidence.

Signed Bailian capability leases

Every remote cloud or e-commerce call carries a time-limited hardware lease locking exact model hashes, spending limits, and narrow tool permissions.

Prompt-fenced GUI automation

Separating trusted user instructions from untrusted supplier emails or QR codes stops hidden prompt injections from seizing local files or payment tools.

Section 05 · Safe ASI Adoption and Macroeconomic Stability

Protecting small merchants, workshops, and gig workers from squeeze

When giant digital marketplaces run stronger pricing and routing models than individual sellers or couriers, small businesses lose bargaining power. Running loyal 1:1 Qwen assistants on local phones and shop laptops gives independent merchants, farmers, and drivers a private contract and pricing shield on their own side.

Backed by Universal Basic Compute, small firms use local Qwen-Coder tools to build custom inventory and bookkeeping software.6 Automated supply hubs share ownership broadly to prevent income concentration7 and settle regional trade through zero-interest mutual credit.
