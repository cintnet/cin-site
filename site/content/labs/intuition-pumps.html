When engineers explain a bridge or an electrical grid, a static blueprint only gets you halfway. To understand why each fuse sits where it does, you need to run a storm through the circuit and watch what trips first.

These four playthroughs work as concrete intuition pumps. Each one steps through a real pressure test: a sudden stock-market credit freeze, a tenfold jump in clinic and workshop speed, a fast frontier-AI race, and an ordinary household week. Toggle any of the five safeguards off at any step to see what breaks without it.

Why scenario playthroughs matter

People often ask whether one safeguard alone could do the job. Could a loyal personal assistant fix everything without a separate agent network? Could mutual-credit clearing replace commercial banks without physical audits?

Pulling the fuses above shows why the pieces interlock. Mutual clearing without physical audits invites exaggerated urgency and hoarding.1,2 Expiring leases without signed receipts leave no proof of who broke a promise, and unmonitored automation erodes human skill and control.3 A loyal personal assistant without public compute still depends on one distant company's cloud.

For researchers

Scenario 01 draws directly from CIN Working Paper M31 (Audited Cycle-Compressed Primal-Dual Clearing), pairing multilateral graph cycle cancellation (Fleischman, 2026, arXiv:2605.02436) with zero-interest WIR/Sardex mutual-credit lines (Stodder, 2009; Iosifidis et al., 2018) and costly physical verification audits (Perez-Richet, 2023) to satisfy Hurwicz (1972) and Myerson-Satterthwaite (1983) incentive constraints. Scenario 02 draws on Bainbridge (1983) and CIN papers M43 and M49 (skill-extinction quotas and fluid role rotation). Scenario 03 models AI control airlocks (Greenblatt et al., 2024) and expiring object-capability leases (Dennis & Van Horn, 1966; draft-cin-agent-network-00). Scenario 04 models private set intersection group purchasing (M40) and selective zero-knowledge residency proofs.
