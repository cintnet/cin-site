Shared Horizon traces five steps from private home sensors and a 1:1 personal AI to organic community self-rule and public computing, backed by our 348-organisation guidance atlas. Today, visibility runs upside down: ordinary families have their clicks, locations, and habits tracked every day while powerful corporations and state agencies make high-stakes AI decisions behind closed doors.

Our open architecture flips that arrangement. Personal life stays private at home, and your local AI builds whatever software you need without monopoly rent. Communities then organize naturally across shared ledgers backed by public computing power.

How it works in five steps

Select any station in the diagram below, or use the Next and Previous buttons, to trace how data moves from your home to community self-rule.

Step 1 of 5
Step 1 of 5 · Private Capture

Honest sensing and your home vault

Wearable glasses, watches, and room sensors help your system notice daily life. They save those memories inside an encrypted vault on hardware in your own home, never on an advertising cloud.

Physical sensors can still be tricked by glare, noise, or tampering. Instead of treating one reading as automatic truth, your vault cross-checks sensors and records how sure each observation actually is.1,2

In everyday terms. Keep your diary and home camera archive in a locked cabinet at home, with an honest note whenever a window was fogged up.

What stays under your control

Your raw health, voice, location, and daily history stay encrypted strictly for you and your personal model.

Why this changes the rules

Tech platforms lose their silent feed of family data. A single faulty sensor reading can never automatically penalize you.

Scientific terminology & primary sources

Grounded in hardware root-of-trust peripheral attestation (DMTF SPDM, IETF RATS), append-only C2PA Content Credentials3, and transduction security across the analog hole. Kevin Fu and Wenyuan Xu (2018) show why digital signatures after analog capture cannot guarantee physical truth when signals are spoofed upstream (doi:10.1145/3176402). Kun Wang et al. (2026) extend this analysis to analog hardware sensor trojans (arXiv:2611.06103).1,2

Step 2 of 5 · Personal Intelligence & Custom Tools

A 1:1 local AI that builds any software you need

One personal AI model works solely for you, bound to your wishes like a family doctor. It steps back so your own skills stay sharp, and says plainly when it is unsure.

Capable local agents can also write, test, and run custom software of almost any complexity right inside a safe sandbox on your own hardware.4,5 Households, clinics, and small shops no longer have to pay endless monthly software rent or accept rigid apps.6

In everyday terms. Instead of renting pre-made tools from a company store that watches you work, a skilled craftsperson in your corner builds the exact tool you need.

What stays under your control

Your assistant answers to zero advertisers. Every custom program it builds runs locally under permissions you can inspect and turn off.

Why this changes the rules

When any person or cooperative can recreate complex software on demand, software monopolies lose their lock-in and artificial paywalls.

Scientific terminology & primary sources

Combines principal-aligned information fiduciaries with local-first malleable software (Martin Kleppmann et al., 2019, doi:10.1145/3359591.3359737) and capability-sandboxed program synthesis (WASI). Ripon Chandra Malo, Zimeng Yu, and Tong Qiu (2026) show empirically how coding agents shift software from rented SaaS subscriptions to user-built local tools (arXiv:2610.30307). Thomas Kwa et al. (2025, arXiv:2503.14499) measure expanding software task horizons, while Eugene Bagdasarian et al. (2024, arXiv:2405.05175) bound context leakage.4,5,6,7

Step 3 of 5 · Crossing the Boundary Safely

Coordinating across private and shared ledgers

To work with the outside world without exposing your life, your assistant keeps two separate books. Your private ledger stays inside your home and holds your raw memories, health notes, and finances.

When you book a service, verify a license, or share work, your assistant talks to a shared remote ledger across a strict privacy boundary.8 It shares only the minimum mathematical proof needed for that one task, using a temporary permission that expires automatically.9,10

In everyday terms. Prove at a doorway that you are old enough to enter, without handing over your home address, diary, or bank history.

What stays under your control

Outside software never gets direct access to your home vault or permanent keys to your accounts.

Why this changes the rules

People can verify facts, settle agreements, and hold outside agents to tamper-evident records without surrendering personal privacy.

Scientific terminology & primary sources

Implements a statistical Markov blanket separating internal state from external network state (Karl Friston, 2013, doi:10.1098/rsif.2013.0475), paired with zero-knowledge proofs (Haochen Sun et al., 2024, arXiv:2404.16109). Peer-to-peer verified shared records follow David Hyland-Wood and Rob Ewin (2026, arXiv:2611.06034), bounded by selective online containment airlocks (Zhonghao Zhan et al., 2026, arXiv:2611.06727).8,9,10,11

Step 4 of 5 · Bottom-Up Collective Action

How communities organize organically across shared ledgers

Real communities connect neighbours, small shops, unions, and public agencies who hold different levels of trust. Multi-trust ledgers let them verify shared facts, build joint software, and pool buying power without a central corporate boss.12,13

Because each person keeps their own boundary while linking shared rules, alliances form naturally around common interests.14 Home life keeps zero public exposure, while actions carrying high risk to society require open proof.

In everyday terms. Independent grocers, local farms, and town clinics pool delivery routes and wholesale orders on a shared logbook while staying independent.

What stays under your control

Communities write their own local rules from the ground up, and every member can leave with their records intact.

Why this changes the rules

High-risk labs, monopolies, and agencies can no longer play isolated citizens off against each other. Operating at scale requires open proof.

Scientific terminology & primary sources

Draws on polycentric governance of complex economic systems (Elinor Ostrom, 2010 Nobel Lecture, doi:10.1257/aer.100.3.641) and scale-free collective intelligence across bounded subunits (Michael Levin, 2019, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02688). Formal accountability bounds follow Arin Mukhopadhyay and Aris Filos-Ratsikas (2026, arXiv:2610.21746), alongside cyclic mutual-credit clearing (George Iosifidis et al., 2018, doi:10.1038/s41562-018-0450-0).12,13,14,15

Step 5 of 5 · Essential Civic Infrastructure

Why public computing and universal basic compute matter

Open software blueprints mean little if three giant companies own all the data centers and chips required to run them.16 Economists warn that cash handouts alone fail if families must spend every dollar renting intelligence back from cloud monopolies.17,18

Treating core computing power like public libraries, roads, or clean water changes that equation. A guaranteed baseline of universal basic compute and regional public clusters gives households, co-ops, clinics, and independent safety auditors real capacity of their own.19

In everyday terms. Public roads and municipal electricity mean a local bakery or clinic never has to beg a conglomerate to turn on the lights.

What stays under your control

Guaranteed baseline compute lets households run capable local agents, and lets independent scientists audit frontier AI without corporate charity.

Why this changes the rules

Preventing chip and cloud enclosure stops monopolies from becoming permanent toll collectors over human thought and economic life.

Scientific terminology & primary sources

Girish Sastry et al. (2024, arXiv:2402.08797) establish compute as a primary governance lever for frontier AI, while Nur Ahmed and Muntasir Wahed (2020, arXiv:2010.15581) measure the compute divide. Anton Korinek and Joseph Stiglitz (2017, doi:10.3386/w24174) and Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo (2019, doi:10.1257/jep.33.2.3) show why automation without broad productive ownership concentrates rents, motivating public compute utilities (NSF NAIRR, EuroHPC) and Universal Basic Compute.16,17,18,19

Where shared interests meet in three real-world cases

Concentrated power rarely gives up control out of goodwill. Select a challenge below to see why isolated people lose today, who shares a stake, and how the five steps flip the incentive.

1. Why people lose alone

The race-to-deploy trap

A few labs hold the largest compute clusters and launch autonomous systems behind trade secrets. Inside engineers risk firing if they speak up, outside auditors depend on donated credits, and citizens cannot check what is safe.

2. How the five steps connect them

Local boundaries and public testbeds

Public compute gives independent researchers hardware of their own to stress-test models. Meanwhile, 1:1 household and business assistants automatically reject outside agent calls that lack verifiable safety proofs.

3. Why concentrated power yields

Openness becomes the price of entry

Public agencies, businesses, and personal assistants all require reproducible safety records on the shared ledger before high-risk software can transact. Keeping a model inside a black box locks it out of adoption.

Who shares a direct stake in Case 01

Conscientious engineers inside AI labs

Shared stake: Want enforceable safety red lines so launch pressure cannot bury dangerous test results.

Practical move: Back protected whistleblower disclosures and pre-deployment capability commitments tied to checkable audit records.

Independent safety & security researchers

Shared stake: Need guaranteed public compute so an honest safety critique never costs them next quarter's server grant.

Practical move: Run reproducible containment and deception tests on public clusters and publish open results.

Households, hospitals & local businesses

Shared stake: Refuse to be guinea pigs for autonomous software that can move money or fail without a human off-switch.

Practical move: Set local 1:1 assistants to block unverified outside agents and enforce temporary permissions.

Public procurement & infrastructure officials

Shared stake: Carry legal and public responsibility when uninspected software fails inside power grids, courts, or hospitals.

Practical move: Require independent safety proofs and named human accountability before high-risk models clear public contracts.

Three paths over the next two years, and what to watch for

Nobody knows which future will win by 2028. We track observable signs every month to see whether AI rules are drifting toward closed clouds or verifiable human oversight.

Path 01 · Unchecked Drift (45%)

Paper checklists and leaky routers

Organizations keep trusting vendor privacy checkboxes and human review of long machine logs, even when tests miss subtle errors or middlemen retain prompts.

What to watch: More silent data leaks across API resellers and automated safety audits passed on self-graded scores without independent re-runs.

Path 02 · Closed Lock-In (35%)

Giant walled clouds cut out outsiders

After public lawsuits and leaks, big labs pull tools behind closed corporate walls and strict non-disclosure rules. Leaks slow down, yet outside safety groups lose access to verify anything.

What to watch: Bans on independent testing, rising compute tolls, and safety staff still needing to resign to warn the public.

Path 03 · Shared Safeguards (20%)

Local vaults, canary checks, and open receipts

Labs, public buyers, and households pair local 1:1 software with hardware walls, canary checks, and signed logs on public clusters.

What to watch by 2028: Safety institutes requiring blinded repeat runs, public tenders checking decoy canaries, and 25% of open audits running on local or public compute.
