Accountable AI governance pairs statutory frontier airlocks, shared public compute, encrypted single-user privacy, and cross-bloc treaties while rejecting private lobbying capture and mass surveillance. Before a city connects high-voltage substations to neighbourhood homes, municipal inspectors test the circuit breakers at the fence line and seal the meter. No honest utility lets power-plant owners grade their own safety valves in secret, or demands a master key to your house.

Public AI governance faces that same test across twenty leading national jurisdictions and ten global standards and treaty bodies. In some capitals, cloud and defense lobbyists push governments toward voluntary corporate self-grading while keeping agency procurement rules hidden behind trade secrets.1,2 In other capitals, strong safety registries come bundled with real-name surveillance, mandatory decryption backdoors, or sudden regional internet shutdowns.

We evaluate each authority across five public-interest safeguards, spanning statutory safety airlocks, anti-capture appeals, citizen privacy, public compute commons, and cross-bloc treaties.4,5 Every score defends a clear household boundary where one encrypted home memory vault and one loyal assistant serve one living person.3,6
